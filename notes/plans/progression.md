The idea here is to have sequences of things you would have to do (like the chain in Spelunky 2) but also knowledge gates at various intervals, essentially letting you save your progress between runs, because your progress would be in the form of knowledge.

Of the games I've played, Noita seems like the best example of this, but that might just be because it's the only one I played without spoiling myself on the secret ending.

One thing I've realized by making the lists below is that it's hard to hide something if you don't have many things in the game. For example:
- If the Eggplant was the only item in Spelunky, it would be much easier to figure out what to do with it.
- If the Guide Voodoo Doll was the only item in Terraria, it would be much easier to figure out its significance.
- In Celeste, there aren't many ways to interact with the world, and so Celeste doesn't have many good secrets; it has to resort to weird puzzles for the Crystal Hearts.
- Noita has a world with lots of empty space both underground and in the sky, so there's plenty of space to hide secret areas.

In other words, it's hard to hide a needle in a haystack if your haystack only has one grain of hay. In order to have good secrets, you need either a big world, a world that can be interacted with in many different ways, or many different types of items that can be used. In all of these cases, you need to already have a lot of content before starting to develop secrets, so I should probably add more stuff to the main path before thinking more about secrets. When I do start making secrets, I should plan on making just a few alternate levels first (e.g. the floating islands), and only then adding even more well-hidden stuff.

# Ideas for Progression and Examples in Other Games
In the following list, ideas that I like (usually because they're at least somewhat original) are marked with a `[!]`.

Ways to progress that do not already require having a secret item or being in a secret location:
- You can **find a guaranteed item slightly out of the way** like the Udjat Eye or Hou Yi's Bow in Spelunky.
- You can **find various random, potentially-useful items** like how you might happen to find digging wands or teleport bolts in Noita, which can let you access secret areas.
- You can **defeat a guaranteed but optional boss** like Anubis in Spelunky HD, or like the dream bosses in Hollow Knight.
- You can **fight optional phases of a non-optional boss** like when you get the Ankh from Olmec in Spelunky 2.
- `[!]` You can **exploit enemy behavior to destroy an obstacle**.
- `[!]` You can **exploit enemy behavior to increase mobility**.
- `[!]` You can **get a certain combination of enemies to meet each other**, like how in my game if you kite all the triangle people into the same room, they will disassemble themselves and reassemble into a boss for you to fight.
- You can **defeat all the enemies in a region**, which is especially secret if the player can't harm them directly and has to use the environment or the enemies' own attacks.

Ways to use an item you've obtained to continue towards the secret ending:
- You can **use the item to open a door in the background** like the entrances to Hell or the City of Gold in Spelunky, though this is boring.
	- Note: if the door is in an obvious place, this can be a good way to clue the player in to the fact that there are secrets in the game, without telling them what or where the secrets are.
- You can **use the item to destroy a physical barrier** like the drill in Spelunky 2, or like digging wands in Noita.
- You can **use a mobility item to get over an obstacle** like the Qilin in Spelunky 2, or like getting to the floating islands in Noita.
- You can **use an item to neutralize a dangerous substance** like when you cross the lava lake using water in Noita.
	- Example: you could **use an item to neutralize a dangerous toxic gas** and get to a secret area.
	- Example: you could **use an item to gain immunity to an otherwise-toxic solid** that would otherwise prevent you from easily exploring an area, since you wouldn't be able to touch the ground for more than a few seconds without dying otherwise.
	- Example: you could **use an item to gain immunity to electricity passing through a liquid** that would otherwise kill you 
- You can **defeat an enemy using a particular special weapon**, like when you shoot Hundun with the Arrow of Light in Spelunky 2.

Ways to use information:
- You can **use information to locate a tile hidden behind a block**, like using the Udjat Eye to find the Black Market in Spelunky 2.
	- `[!]` In my game it could be a secret room, hidden by a disappearing wall like in Celeste.
- You can **use information to choose one object out of many**, like choosing the Ushabti containing the Qilin in Spelunky 2, or choosing the counterclockwise cyclone in Outer Wilds.
- You can **use information to combine several objects out of many**, like mixing potions in Noita.
- You can **assemble pieces of a code to decode a language**, like the language in the instruction manual in Tunic. (I don't like this mechanic though).
	- `[!]` I could make it so when you go to a new area, the name appears on screen in the unknown language and then a moment later the letters convert to ordinary letters of the alphabet. Then there could be, say, a fountain, with some symbols in the unknown language, and when you enter the code a door opens.
	  
	  I want this to be more of a knowledge check than a puzzle, so it should be fairly easy to figure out what to do. In particular, I should have the runes change colors to show progress on entering the code whenever the user presses a key (even one that is bound to an input). Then once they enter the wrong code, it will flash red. This should clue them into the fact that it's a code, which should then prompt them to try keys that aren't bound to any input. Then they should be able to figure out the rest from there.
- You can **use information to dig at a certain nondescript location**, like treasure maps in Minecraft, or like the secret to the right of Snowy Depths in Noita.
- You can **enter a sequence of directions at a particular location**, like all the Holy Cross puzzles in Tunic.
	- If I decided to do this, I could use the up and down arrow keys, since they aren't used for much.
- You can **stand very still at a precise location**, like with the Mother Statue in Spelunky 2.
- You can **enter a non-numeric code**, like the Eye coordinates in Outer Wilds.
- You could **enter a word at a text input** (either on a menu, anywhere, or at a particular location), kind of like in Type Help or like Sofanthiel in Rain World.
- `[!]` You can **enter a sequence of directions by going through gates** which is good because this will be tricky to do even if you know the code.
